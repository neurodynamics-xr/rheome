rheome.geom.ladder

function rheome.geom — Rheome

  GEOM.LADDER  The spatial ladder: which eigenvalue range each cortical tile level corresponds to.
 
    L = rheome.geom.ladder('sub01')
    L = rheome.geom.ladder(name, MaxDepth=6, Cycles=2)
 
  The cortical twin of `rheome.select.ladder`. That one answers "at this frequency band, how long is a tile,
  how many cycles is that, and at what rate does the band arrive?" and finds the two invariants that
  make the time-frequency plane work: cycles per tile is the same in every octave (22.6 on the reference
  bank) and samples per tile is too. This answers the same question on the spatial axes, and the
  structure is the same with one factor of two that has to be got right.
 
  ⭐⭐ ONE SPATIAL OCTAVE IS **TWO** TREE DEPTHS, NOT ONE. `rheome.geom.tree` bisects by AREA, so area halves
  every depth and DIAMETER falls by sqrt(2). Measured on this cortex: 278 / 196 / 139 / 98 mm at
  depths 1-4, ratios 1.42 / 1.41 / 1.42. A factor of two in wavelength therefore costs two levels of
  the position pyramid, where a factor of two in period costs one level of the time pyramid. Treating
  the two axes as level-for-level is off by a factor of two per octave and compounds.
 
  ⭐ THE INVARIANT THAT MIRRORS CONSTANT-Q. By Weyl's law the number of eigenvalues below k on a
  surface of area A is N(k) = A k^2 / (4 pi), so an octave [k, 2k] holds 3 A k^2 / (16 pi) modes --
  a factor of 4 per octave. A node at depth D has area A0 / 2^D. So the modes that fit inside one
  node in one octave go as 2^-D k^2, which is CONSTANT exactly when D increases by 2 per octave.
  Follow that diagonal and every (tile, octave) cell holds the same number of modes, just as every
  (tile, band) cell holds the same number of cycles. That is what makes the cell a unit of
  information rather than an arbitrary intersection.
 
  ⚠⚠ THE OFF-DIAGONAL IS NOT SYMMETRIC. Above the diagonal (node much larger than the wavelength) a
  cell is merely coarse: many modes averaged, no localisation gained, still a valid measurement.
  BELOW it (node smaller than the wavelength) the cell is not a measurement at all -- a mode whose
  wavelength exceeds the patch does not complete a cycle inside it, so its "energy in this patch" is
  an artefact of where the patch was drawn. `.forbidden` marks those.
 
  ⚠⚠ WHAT MAY BE RECORDED IN SUCH A CELL, AND WHAT MAY NOT.
    ⭐ DO record band-limited energy: filter the current to the octave (linear and exact in the
      Dirac or LB basis), then integrate |J|^2 over the node. Well defined, and it rolls up over
      nodes within a fixed octave.
    ⚠ DO NOT record "eigenmode bandpower of this node" in the sense of apportioning global modes to
      patches. An eigenmode has support over the whole hemisphere; "how much of mode m lives in node
      n" has no answer without a convention, and different conventions disagree. Global eigenmode
      bandpower (summed over the hemisphere) IS well defined -- that is the `space_band` /
      feature_space pattern the schema already uses on the sensor graph -- but it has no node index.
    ⚠ DO NOT record a SIZE. A planting test planted 70-267 mm through the leadfield, noise and
      inverse: recovered ~100 mm regardless, slope +0.02, R^2 0.002 below 10 dB. The octave index
      says which filter responded, never how big the source was.
 
  ⚠ `Cycles` is the spatial twin of cycles-per-tile and its value is NOT assumed: the default 2 is a
  starting point and the table reports the achieved ratio per row so it can be read off, exactly as
  rheome.select.ladder reports 22.6 rather than asserting it.
 
  OUTPUT (table L, one row per spatial octave)
    sband, wavelengthLoMM/HiMM/CenterMM, kLo/kHi/kCenter, nModes (counted), nModesWeyl (predicted),
    naturalDepth, nodeDiameterMM, nNodes, cyclesPerTile, modesPerCell, nodeAdmissible,
    waveAdmissible, forbidden
 
  ⚠⚠ TWO SEPARATE ADMISSIBILITY QUESTIONS, and conflating them overstates what the plane supports.
    nodeAdmissible  is the TILE bigger than 2*r50 = 104 mm? -- can this patch be told from its
                    neighbour at all (rheome.inverse.resolution, 300 seeds)
    waveAdmissible  is the WAVELENGTH above 104 mm? -- a separate and stricter question, since
                    planting recovers ~100 mm whatever is planted below 10 dB
  A cell needs BOTH, plus `~forbidden`. ⭐⭐ MEASURED ON THIS CORTEX, AT Cycles=2, NOTHING QUALIFIES,
  and the way it fails is the result. The only candidate is the 94-189 mm octave (centre 134 mm),
  which wants a tile of 2 x 133.7 = 267.4 mm; the largest tile on the hemisphere is 267.2 mm. It
  misses by 0.2 mm, or 0.1%. Every other octave fails on the wavelength floor instead.
 
  So the answer to "does a cortical tile have a corresponding eigenvalue range" is YES, exactly and
  structurally -- and with this array the (position x spatial-frequency) plane collapses to AT MOST
  ONE cell, sitting exactly on its own boundary. Whether it counts is decided by `Cycles`, a
  convention nobody has measured. That is not a defect in the ladder; it is the same fact as
  trace(R) = 48-113 of 270 channels and r50 = 52 mm, seen on a different pair of axes.
 
  ⭐ THE CONTRAST WITH TIME IS THE POINT. rheome.select.ladder gets 22.6 CYCLES per time tile in every
  octave. The spatial axis manages about 2 diameters per wavelength before running out of cortex --
  an order of magnitude poorer. The time-frequency plane is rich and the space-wavenumber plane is
  barely one cell, and that asymmetry should be read off this table rather than assumed away.
 
  ⚠ `forbidden` is also TRUE for the coarsest octave, and that is correct rather than a bug: the
  189-378 mm octave needs a tile of 2 x 267 = 534 mm, larger than the 378 mm hemisphere. The largest
  spatial octave therefore has no tile at all and exists only as a whole-hemisphere number.
 
  See also: rheome.select.ladder, rheome.geom.tree, rheome.flow.sweep
